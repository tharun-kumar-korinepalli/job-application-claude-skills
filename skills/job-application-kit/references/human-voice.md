# Making it sound like a person

Recruiters and hiring managers now read hundreds of AI-written letters a week. They recognise the shape in two sentences and skip. A letter that gets read sounds like one specific person who wanted this specific job. This file is a check to run over every piece of prose before delivering it: cover letters, motivation letters, summaries, emails, LinkedIn messages.

Based on Wikipedia's "Signs of AI writing" and the humanizer skill, cut down to what matters for application documents.

## The rules, strongest first

1. No "not X but Y". "I am not just a coder, I am a problem solver." Say the claim. "I fix the problems that show up between teams."

2. No one-line dramatic closer. "That is what I bring." "And that is the difference." Cut the line.

3. No sayings that sound deep. "At its core", "what really matters", "the real question is", "X is the currency of Y". State the fact.

4. No staged run-up. "Let me be direct." "Here is the thing." "I will be honest." Just say it.

5. No arguing with nobody. "I am not saying I am perfect, but". "Some might think a teacher cannot". Delete the imagined objection.

6. No forced triads. "Fast, reliable and scalable." "I build, I ship, I learn." If there are two things, say two. If four, say four.

7. No em dashes. None. Use a comma, a full stop, or brackets. Hyphens only inside words.

8. No inflated claims. "Proven track record", "extensive experience", "deep expertise", "passionate about", "thrilled", "excited", "delighted", "honoured", "leverage", "synergy", "cutting-edge", "state-of-the-art", "world-class", "seamless", "robust", "holistic", "dynamic", "innovative", "impactful", "spearheaded", "empower", "elevate", "unlock", "navigate", "landscape", "journey", "tapestry", "testament", "delve", "foster", "cultivate", "harness", "underscore", "pivotal", "crucial", "vital", "game-changer", "in today's fast-paced world", "ever-evolving". Replace with a plain word or a fact.

9. No bold labels or headings inside a letter. No bullet lists in a cover letter.

10. No stock openers or closers. "I hope this message finds you well." "I am writing to express my interest." "Thank you for considering my application." "I look forward to the opportunity to discuss." Open with a fact about them; close with availability and one sentence inviting a conversation.

11. No paragraphs that all start the same way. Vary. Real writing mixes short sentences with longer ones.

12. No "as a [role]" opener. "As an AI researcher, I..." Start with what happened.

13. No filler adverbs. "Genuinely", "truly", "really", "highly", "extremely", "incredibly", "successfully". Cut.

14. No self-assessment. "I am a fast learner with excellent communication skills." Show it: "I picked up Rust in six weeks for the parser rewrite and wrote the internal guide the team still uses."

15. No perfect symmetry. Three paragraphs of the same length with the same structure reads generated. Let one be short.

## Voice

- If the user has written anything themselves (an old letter, an email, a message in the chat), match its rhythm and vocabulary. Their voice beats these rules where the two conflict.
- Contractions are fine in English letters to startups and most tech companies; avoid them for public sector, law, finance and DACH corporate.
- First person, active voice. "I built", not "the system was built".
- One opinion or one specific detail per letter that only this person would write. The product they use. The paper they read. The bug they hit. The city they are moving to. It is the fastest proof of a human author.
- Sentences under 25 words. If a sentence has three commas, split it.
- Plain words. Use, not utilise. Help, not facilitate. Start, not commence. Build, not architect (as a verb).

## The test

Read it aloud. Then ask:
- Could this letter be sent to a different company with the name swapped? If yes, it is not done.
- Would the person say these sentences in an interview without embarrassment? If no, rewrite.
- Is there one line that only this person could have written? If no, find one.
- Is there any sentence that only signals enthusiasm without saying anything? Cut it.

## German

The same rules hold. German filler to cut: "hochmotiviert", "Teamplayer", "belastbar", "Leidenschaft", "mit großem Interesse", "spannende Herausforderung", "zielorientiert", "ich bin überzeugt, dass". German AI text also loves "nicht nur, sondern auch" and "sowohl als auch"; treat them like "not X but Y" and triads.

## Example

Before:
> I am writing to express my genuine interest in the Machine Learning Engineer position. As a passionate and results-driven professional with a proven track record in cutting-edge AI, I am confident that my extensive experience aligns seamlessly with your needs. I am not just a developer; I am a problem solver who thrives in dynamic environments. I would be honoured to bring my skills to your innovative team.

After:
> Your posting asks for someone who has taken a retrieval system from prototype to daily use. At [company] I built one for the support team; it now answers about 300 queries a day and replaced a shared-drive search nobody used. I want to do the same thing at a company where the documents are customer-facing, and your support-automation product is the closest match I have found.

The second version is shorter, has two numbers, names their product, and could not be sent anywhere else.
