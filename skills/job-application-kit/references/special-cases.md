# Special cases

## Career change

- Summary first. Bridge the two worlds in one sentence: "Mechanical engineer turned ML engineer; eight years of industrial process data behind the models I now build."
- Lead with the section that proves the new direction. For most changers that is Projects or a recent degree, not the old experience.
- Rewrite old bullets to surface transferable content. A plant manager who "reduced downtime 20 percent by analysing sensor logs" was doing data work. Say so with the vocabulary of the new field, where it is true.
- Cut old detail that does not serve the new target. The old career becomes two or three lines per role.
- The letter must answer "why the switch" in two sentences, without apology, and name the concrete steps taken (degree, certification, projects, freelance work).
- Do not relabel old job titles into new-field titles. "Data Analyst" for a job that was "Production Planner" is a lie; "Production Planner (data analysis and forecasting)" is not.

## Employment gaps

- Never stretch dates to cover a gap. It is the most common thing background checks catch.
- Under three months: nothing needed.
- Longer: one line on the CV with an honest label (parental leave, caregiving, health, study, relocation, job search after a layoff, travel), plus anything useful done during it (course, freelance, volunteering, open-source).
- One sentence in the letter if the gap is recent or long. No details on health. "I took eighteen months out for family reasons and am fully available from March."
- Years-only dates are sometimes suggested to hide short gaps. Do not recommend it; recruiters read years-only as a signal and ask.

## Layoff

- The resume does not mention it. Dates end when they end.
- The letter can mention it in half a sentence if the timing raises a question: "My role at X ended in the company-wide reduction in June."
- Plenty of people were laid off in 2023 to 2026; recruiters do not hold it against anyone. What they notice is what the person did in the months after.

## Overqualified

- The letter has to answer "why this role" in the first paragraph, directly. Wanting to build again, wanting a smaller company, relocating, changing field, caring responsibilities. Any honest reason works; a missing reason does not.
- Trim the resume to the level of the role. Senior leadership bullets can be shortened; hands-on bullets promoted.
- Do not lower titles. Do not remove degrees.

## Underqualified on one or two must-haves

- Apply if 60 percent or more of must-haves are covered and the gaps are learnable.
- Show the adjacent thing on the resume: no Kubernetes but Docker and ECS; no Rust but C++ and Go.
- Address the gap in the letter in one sentence with a plan, and move on.
- Do not pad the skills list with the missing item after a weekend tutorial.

## Visa and relocation

- Say it early and plainly, because recruiters have to know. Contact line or first sentence of the close: "EU Blue Card, no sponsorship needed", "Requires visa sponsorship", "Relocating to Munich in January; interviewing in person from then."
- If sponsorship is needed and the company does not do it, the application is a no; find out first when the posting is silent.
- Germany specifics (Blue Card thresholds, Chancenkarte, §18 permits) change yearly; do not quote numbers from memory, check the current BAMF/Make-it-in-Germany pages.

## Students and recent graduates

- Education first, with thesis title, grade if good, and relevant coursework.
- Internships, working-student jobs, teaching assistant, research assistant and thesis work count as experience. Give them real bullets with results.
- Projects section with two to three substantial projects, links to code.
- One page. No summary.
- Hackathons, competitions, open-source contributions, publications, scholarships go in a short Awards or Activities line.
- The letter should name the specific team or product and show the person has done something in that direction already.

## Returning after a long break (parental, health, caregiving)

- Label the break honestly, one line, on the CV.
- Add a "Recent" block if there was any relevant activity: course, certification, volunteering, part-time or freelance.
- Emphasise the strongest earlier results; they are still the person's results.
- The letter says the person is back, available, and current, in two sentences.

## Same company, several roles

List the company once, each role underneath with its own dates and bullets. It shows promotion. If the roles were very different, two separate entries are fine.

## Freelance and consulting

One entry "Independent consultant, City, dates" with the two or three most relevant clients or projects as bullets, each with client type, work done, result. Name clients only if allowed.

## Many short jobs

Group contract or agency work under one heading ("Contract software engineer, 2021–2023, via X Agency") with clients as bullets. Do not list eight two-month roles as separate entries; it reads as instability even when it was normal contracting.

## Older candidates

- Cut anything older than fifteen years to one line or drop it.
- No graduation year if it dates the person and the market is known for age bias (US); in DACH it is expected, keep it.
- Recent, current technologies at the top of the skills list.
- Never write "over 25 years of experience" in the summary; say "15+" and let the dates speak.

## Disability, health, protected characteristics

Nothing on the resume unless the person chooses to disclose. In Germany a recognised Schwerbehinderung can be disclosed for the legal advantages it carries (extra leave, protection, quota); it is the person's choice and the choice is theirs alone. Do not advise hiding or altering any protected characteristic to game a process, and do not advise adding one.

## The person wants to exaggerate

Say no, briefly, and offer the honest version. The interview will test every line, references get checked, and in Germany a false statement in application documents can be grounds for dismissal after hiring. The honest version, written well, is almost always strong enough.
