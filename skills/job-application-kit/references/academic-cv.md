# Academic and research CVs

For PhD applications, postdoc, research scientist in a lab, lecturer and professor track, and research-heavy industry roles (research labs at large companies).

## How it differs from a resume

- No page limit. Completeness matters. A senior academic CV can run ten pages.
- Publications are the centre of the document, with full citations.
- Teaching, supervision, funding, service and talks each get their own section.
- The reader is a committee or a professor, not a recruiter. They will read the whole thing, but they will also scan for the research fit in the first thirty seconds. Put the research interests and the strongest publications on page one.
- No photo, no DOB, no marital status. Nationality only if a visa question exists for the position.

## Section order

1. Name, affiliation, contact, links (Google Scholar, ORCID, GitHub, homepage)
2. Research interests: three to five lines, specific. Not "machine learning". "Uncertainty estimation for medical image segmentation; label-efficient learning; evaluation under distribution shift."
3. Education: degrees, institution, year, thesis title, advisor, grade if strong
4. Positions: research associate, visiting, industry research
5. Publications
   - Peer-reviewed journal articles
   - Peer-reviewed conference papers (in CS, these are the main venue; say the venue and its rank if it helps a non-CS reader)
   - Preprints and under review (mark clearly, never present as accepted)
   - Workshop papers, posters, theses
   - Own name in bold; asterisk for equal contribution; corresponding author marked
6. Talks and presentations: invited first, then contributed
7. Grants, awards, scholarships: amount and role (PI, co-PI) where relevant
8. Teaching: course, institution, role (designed, taught, TA), size, term
9. Supervision: theses supervised with level and year, students placed
10. Service: reviewing (list venues), committees, organising
11. Skills: methods, tools, languages, lab equipment
12. Professional memberships
13. References: three to five named, with title, institution, email, and relationship. Academic applications almost always want named referees.

For a PhD application, move Education up, and add: master's thesis summary, research experience with concrete contributions, and any publication or preprint. Grades matter more here.

## Publications format

Use the field's citation style consistently. CS example:

> **Surname, F.**, Coauthor, A., and Advisor, B. (2026). Title of the paper. In *Proceedings of the 2026 Conference on Empirical Methods in Natural Language Processing (EMNLP)*, pp. 1234–1245. [Core A*]

Number them. Most recent first, or chronological within each subsection; be consistent. Include DOI or arXiv link. If there is a long list, add a line at the top with counts: "14 peer-reviewed papers, 6 as first author, h-index 7".

Under review: list under a separate heading "Under review" with venue and date submitted. Never list a rejected or planned paper.

## Research statement and cover letter

Academic applications usually ask for a research statement (two to four pages) separate from the letter. The letter itself is then one page: which position, why this group, one paragraph on the research fit naming the professor's work, one on teaching or methods if relevant, close.

For a PhD application to a specific supervisor:
- Name the papers of theirs you read and what question they raise for you.
- Say what you would want to work on in their group in one concrete paragraph.
- Show you can do it: thesis, publication, code, relevant coursework.
- Practicalities: funding status, start date, language.

Generic admiration for "your groundbreaking research" ends the read.

## German academic conventions

- "Wissenschaftlicher Mitarbeiter" positions at a Hochschule or university: the tabular Lebenslauf is fine, with a publication list attached separately. Zeugnisse are required.
- Habilitation, Professur (W1, W2, W3): the Berufungskommission wants a full CV, publication list, teaching record (Lehrverzeichnis), evaluations, third-party funding (Drittmittel), a research concept and a teaching concept. Follow the Ausschreibung to the letter; missing a listed document is disqualifying.
- Doktorand applications to an industry-linked institute: emphasise applied results, tools shipped, industry partners, and language level.

## Industry research labs

Hybrid: a two-page resume with a "Selected publications" block of four to six entries, then a full list linked or attached. Impact language matters more than in academia. Show deployment, benchmarks, open-source adoption, patents.

## Common mistakes

- Listing preprints as publications without saying so
- Vague research interests
- Teaching listed without numbers or role
- No links to Scholar, ORCID or code
- Referees not warned in advance
- Sending a two-page industry resume to a hiring committee that wanted the full CV, or a ten-page CV to a startup
