# Writing experience bullets

The bullet is the unit of a resume. Recruiters read the first bullet of each role, then decide whether to read the rest. Get those first bullets right.

## The formula

Verb + what you did + context (domain, scale, users) + result (number, or concrete change) + how (tools, method).

Google's version: "Accomplished X, as measured by Y, by doing Z."

Weak:
> Responsible for the recommendation system.

Fine:
> Built the product recommendation service in Python and Spark.

Good:
> Rebuilt the product recommendation service for a 2M-user marketplace, lifting click-through 18 percent, by replacing a rules engine with a two-tower model served on Kubernetes (Python, PyTorch, Spark).

Not every bullet needs a number. Aim for more than half. A bullet with no number should at least carry scope: what system, how many users, which domain, what data.

## Result types that count

- Percentages: reduced, increased, improved by X percent
- Money: saved, generated, managed budget of
- Volume and scale: users, requests per second, records, countries, sites
- Time: cut from three days to two hours, delivered four weeks early
- Quality: error rate, uptime, defect count, review score
- People: team of six, mentored four, trained forty operators
- Adoption: used by eight teams, became the standard for
- Recognition: promoted, chosen to present to the board, award

When the person has no exact number, ask for a defensible estimate and mark it `[confirm]`. When there is no number at all, use scale words: "thousands of daily orders", "the company's main revenue line", "a plant with 300 staff".

## Domain context is the multiplier

Two bullets with the same verb and tool:

> Developed a web application using React and Node.

> Developed the claims-intake portal for a health insurer, handling 40k sensitive medical claims a month under GDPR (React, Node).

The second one tells the reader the industry, the data sensitivity, the scale and the regulation. It makes the person look like a specialist. Always ask: what industry, what data, who used it, what changed.

## Verbs

Strong and plain:
Built, Designed, Led, Shipped, Launched, Migrated, Automated, Reduced, Cut, Increased, Improved, Scaled, Replaced, Rewrote, Debugged, Fixed, Owned, Ran, Trained, Deployed, Integrated, Negotiated, Published, Presented, Taught, Mentored, Hired, Audited, Standardised, Measured, Forecast, Modelled.

Avoid. They sound inflated and recruiters notice:
Spearheaded, Orchestrated, Leveraged, Utilised, Synergised, Championed, Pioneered, Revolutionised, Transformed, Crafted, Honed, Elevated, Empowered, Fostered, Engineered (as a generic verb), Innovated, Masterminded, Drove (as in "drove results").

Never open a bullet with:
Responsible for, Helped, Assisted, Worked on, Participated in, Was involved in, Tasked with, Duties included.

Tense: past tense for past roles. Current role: present or past, but pick one and hold it within the role.

## Format rules

- One to two lines. Three at most. Longer bullets split into two.
- No full stop at the end. Bullets are not sentences.
- Digits, not words: 8 not eight, 30 percent not thirty percent. Write "percent" or "%" consistently.
- No pronouns: no I, we, my, our.
- No sub-bullets.
- Order bullets within a role by relevance to the target posting, then by impressiveness.
- A bullet that wraps to a second line with only one or two words on it wastes a line. Trim or extend.
- Vary the opening verb. Three bullets starting with "Built" in a row look lazy.

## How many bullets

| Role | Bullets |
|---|---|
| Current or most relevant | 4 to 6 |
| Previous, within 3 years | 3 to 5 |
| 3 to 7 years ago | 2 to 3 |
| Older or irrelevant | 1, or fold into one "Earlier roles" line |

## Rewriting someone's existing bullets

Ask before rewriting: what exactly did you do here, and what was different afterward? Most people write their job description instead of their results. The interview usually surfaces a real result within a minute.

Original: "Managed social media accounts."
Ask: How many accounts, what platforms, what happened to follower count, engagement, leads?
Rewrite: "Ran LinkedIn and Instagram for a B2B SaaS, growing followers 4x to 22k in 14 months and sourcing 30 percent of inbound demo requests."

## Things that never go in a bullet

Salary, reasons for leaving, conflicts, failures framed as failures, internal codenames nobody outside the company knows, "successfully" (nobody lists failures), "various" or "multiple" (name them or count them), self-assessment ("excellent communicator"), obvious tools ("Microsoft Word").

## Bullets for non-engineering roles

The formula holds. Examples:

Sales: "Closed EUR 1.4M new ARR in FY25 against a EUR 1.1M quota, mostly mid-market manufacturing accounts in DACH."

Operations: "Cut order-to-ship time from 5 days to 2 by redesigning the picking route and retraining 25 warehouse staff."

Research: "First-authored a NeurIPS 2025 paper on contradiction detection in retrieval pipelines; released code and dataset, 140 GitHub stars."

Teaching: "Designed and taught a 12-week representation learning course for 35 master's students, rated 4.6/5."

Nursing: "Managed a 28-bed surgical ward on night shift, coordinating four nurses and two care assistants, zero medication incidents over 18 months."

## Bullets in German

Same rules. Verbs in the past tense or as nominal style is common but action-style reads stronger and is now accepted:

> Aufbau einer Recommendation-Pipeline für einen Online-Marktplatz mit 2 Mio. Nutzern; Steigerung der Klickrate um 18 % (Python, PyTorch, Spark)

Technical terms stay in English. Numbers use German formatting (1,5 Mio., 18 %). See germany-dach.md.
